# ABYSSAL WAKE — First 10 Hours Campaign Blueprint V1

Status: Director campaign blueprint
Runtime authority: None
Scope: Long-form pacing target for the opening campaign, designed to preserve gradual civilization decline while proving HOME/OUTSIDE progression.

## 1. Purpose

ABYSSAL WAKE should not begin after the apocalypse is already complete.

The first ten hours should let the player experience a recognizable modern world becoming less stable in layers while their HOME becomes more useful in response.

This blueprint is not a quest script and does not authorize immediate implementation of all listed content. It is a pacing map that prevents the team from spending late-game horror too early.

## 2. Opening campaign promise

By Hour 10, the player should have experienced:

- a functional Safe Camp becoming a real HOME
- at least several expeditions with distinct purposes
- multiple modern POIs in different states of decline
- one or two meaningful HOME upgrades
- a small but real equipment/crafting progression
- a reason to care about several survivors
- the first undeniable evidence that the crisis is not normal
- at least one major world beat
- the first clear hint of deeper routes / a future boss-scale threat

The player should **not** yet feel that every mystery is explained.

## 3. Pacing philosophy

The campaign opens with three simultaneous arcs.

### Human Arc

`temporary safety -> useful routines -> service roles -> local community`

### Civilization Arc

`normal services -> stress -> shortage -> closure -> fragmentation`

### Cosmic Arc

`rumor -> anomaly -> contradiction -> recurring pattern -> undeniable impossibility`

These arcs move at different speeds.

The cosmic arc should be the slowest at first.

## 4. Hour 0–1 — Arrival / Ordinary Problems

### Emotional goal

The world still feels like a place where normal life could resume.

### HOME

Safe Camp is basic:
- fire/rest
- temporary storage
- improvised shelter
- unreliable information

### OUTSIDE

Nearby roads and buildings still look modern and recently used.

### Primary problem

Ordinary shortage:
- food
- medicine
- batteries
- tools

### First destination

MIRE MART.

### Horror level

Very low.

Signals:
- odd power flicker
- damaged emergency notice
- abandoned personal objects
- one strange sound or visual contradiction

### Gameplay proof

The player learns:
- preparation
- movement/combat basics
- salvage
- return-to-HOME logic

### Do not reveal

- full cosmic entities
- large-scale mutation
- explicit cult mythology
- giant anomaly structures

## 5. Hour 1–2 — First Useful Return

### Emotional goal

Returning matters.

### HOME response

The first expedition should allow one practical result.

Candidate:
- workbench repair
- generator repair
- medical station repair

Only one needs to exist in the first playable slice.

### Player realization

> Going outside is not just looting. What I bring back changes what we can do here.

### Narrative layer

NPC/service presence begins to matter.

Possible survivor roles introduced lightly:
- mechanic
- medic
- scavenger

They should first appear as people with immediate practical problems, not lore encyclopedias.

## 6. Hour 2–3 — The World Is Still Responding

### Civilization state

Institutions still exist.

Signals:
- emergency radio broadcast
- temporary road closure
- clinic queue/evacuation notice
- utility crews or abandoned utility equipment

### New destination candidate

Clinic / pharmacy route.

### Gameplay purpose

Introduce a destination with a different extraction value:
- medical supplies
- records
- specialized components

### Capability pressure

The player begins to understand that one generic loadout is not ideal for every trip.

Examples:
- more medicine for longer route
- light for dark interior
- carrying choice for valuable bulky salvage

### Horror level

Still explainable as crisis/disaster.

## 7. Hour 3–4 — Shortage and Contradiction

### Civilization state

Supply systems begin failing visibly.

Examples:
- shelves empty
- fuel unavailable
- grid instability
- blocked roads
- misinformation/conflicting reports

### Cosmic signal

Two separate ordinary explanations contradict each other.

Example:
- official report says contamination
- clinic records show symptoms that do not fit

The player should not yet know what the correct explanation is.

### HOME response

Power or medical capability becomes more strategically valuable.

### New progression choice

Offer a lateral decision:

- improve power
- improve medical resilience
- improve extraction/logistics

The exact implementation can remain linear in early builds, but the design target is choice.

## 8. Hour 4–5 — First Revisit With Meaning

### Core design test

Return to a known place and find it changed.

Recommended candidate:

MIRE MART.

Possible state change:
- power fully failed
- interior colder/darker
- a survivor has passed through
- different loot remains
- one new locked/powered interaction
- anomaly evidence becomes slightly stronger

### Player realization

> The map is not static between visits.

### Gameplay value

This is the first proof that world state, not just player gear, progresses.

## 9. Hour 5–6 — Community Becomes a System

### HOME

Safe Camp begins to feel inhabited.

Add one or two service loops, not a town simulator.

Potential roles:
- mechanic: power/tools
- medic: recovery/medical crafting
- scavenger: route information/salvage conversion
- quartermaster: expedition preparation/logistics

### Design rule

Each NPC must change at least one player decision.

### Social storytelling

NPCs talk about practical losses:
- family unreachable
- workplace closed
- road blocked
- medicine running out

Keep cosmic exposition minimal.

## 10. Hour 6–7 — Local Disorder

### Civilization state

The problem is now visibly social as well as environmental.

Signals:
- abandoned vehicles
- looting
- makeshift checkpoints
- small survivor groups
- intermittent communications

### New destination candidate

Gas station / utility substation / warehouse.

### Gameplay purpose

Introduce infrastructure salvage:
- fuel
- electrical parts
- mechanical parts

### Capability gate

A repaired generator or access tool now creates a clear advantage.

## 11. Hour 7–8 — The First Impossible Pattern

### Cosmic escalation

The player encounters something that cannot be comfortably explained by disaster.

Examples:
- route distance does not match map
- repeated signage with impossible wording
- sound source moves against geometry
- residue appears in sealed area
- two independent witnesses describe the same dream

### Rule

Do not explain it immediately.

### Gameplay consequence

The impossible phenomenon should alter one decision:
- route timing
- safe distance
- required light/tool
- resource value

This is the first step toward anomaly mechanics.

## 12. Hour 8–9 — Institutional Retreat

### Civilization state

Formal systems stop being dependable locally.

Signals:
- radio stops carrying normal service information
- clinic closes or relocates
- power becomes intermittent
- authorities abandon a checkpoint

### HOME response

The camp must replace one lost public service.

Strong candidates:
- reliable power
- medical care
- communications

### Player realization

> HOME is no longer a temporary camp. It is becoming a replacement for the systems around us.

## 13. Hour 9–10 — End of Opening Arc

### World Beat

The opening arc ends with a major but still restrained transition.

Recommended shape:

- regional blackout / infrastructure failure
- one known POI visibly changes
- new dangerous route becomes relevant
- radio/news narrative changes tone
- enemy/anomaly presence increases locally

### Cosmic reveal level

The player now knows:

- the crisis is not ordinary
- the abnormalities are connected
- something is changing places and living things

The player does **not** yet know:

- full cosmic origin
- final antagonist
- exact nature of the Abyss
- complete endgame stakes

### Hook for next arc

Reveal one of:
- underground service route
- sealed coastal facility
- damaged transit tunnel
- offshore/dock anomaly signal
- survivor report from a cut-off district

## 14. First ten-hour POI network target

Do not build all at once.

A strong eventual opening network might include:

1. Safe Camp / HOME
2. MIRE MART
3. clinic/pharmacy
4. gas station / roadside service
5. utility/substation
6. small apartment or survivor shelter
7. transit/underground entrance

Each must have a distinct purpose.

### MIRE MART

Purpose:
- first accessible salvage
- food/medicine/batteries
- crawler encounter
- first revisit candidate

### Clinic / Pharmacy

Purpose:
- medical progression
- institutional stress storytelling
- records/evidence

### Gas Station / Roadside Service

Purpose:
- fuel/mechanical/logistics
- road-control storytelling

### Utility / Substation

Purpose:
- power capability
- infrastructure mystery
- world beat trigger candidate

### Apartment / Shelter

Purpose:
- human consequences
- survivor/NPC discovery
- domestic environmental storytelling

### Transit / Underground

Purpose:
- later access gate
- first meaningful descent
- bridge into deeper horror

## 15. Quest topology

Avoid one endless mandatory quest chain.

Use three layers.

### Main Pressure

A small number of campaign-driving needs.

Examples:
- stabilize HOME
- investigate repeated failures
- restore essential service

### Expedition Objectives

Concrete goals tied to destinations.

Examples:
- retrieve medicine
- recover generator parts
- inspect closed substation

### Self-Directed Needs

Player-created goals from system pressure.

Examples:
- find more food
- improve light
- recover dropped cargo
- stock medical supplies

As the game matures, self-directed goals should occupy more of the player's time.

## 16. Narrative delivery mix

Target a mixed method.

### Environmental

- changed shelves
- abandoned vehicles
- notices
- repaired/failed infrastructure
- domestic scenes

### Broadcast / Communications

- emergency radio
- local messages
- survivor reports

### NPC

- practical concerns first
- memory and personal stakes
- conflicting interpretations

### Documents / Records

Use sparingly and keep short.

### Systemic Change

Strongest method:

A place behaves differently than before.

## 17. NPC opening cast target

Keep the initial recurring cast small enough to remember.

Suggested functional archetypes:

### Mechanic

Represents infrastructure adaptation.

Arc:
- ordinary repair work
- generator/tool role
- family/communications concern
- later distrust of impossible electrical behavior

### Medic

Represents institutional collapse and human cost.

Arc:
- conventional treatment
- shortage management
- unexplained symptoms
- later contamination questions

### Scavenger / Guide

Represents local knowledge.

Arc:
- knows routes and shortcuts
- tracks closures and survivor rumors
- becomes less confident as geography changes

### Quartermaster / Organizer

Represents community formation.

Arc:
- manages practical shortages
- turns salvage into planning
- later coordinates defense/logistics

Do not introduce all simultaneously if it dilutes attachment.

## 18. Enemy opening ecology

Enemy progression should support ambiguity.

### Early

- crawler
- infected/wet humanoid
- dangerous animal or subtly altered creature

### Mid-opening

- variants with changed behavior
- humans affected in uncertain ways
- small aberration

### Late-opening

- first clearly impossible organism or event-linked entity

Do not show endgame monster silhouettes during the first hour.

## 19. Equipment opening progression

The first ten hours should prioritize functional breadth.

Suggested sequence:

1. basic knife
2. reliable light
3. better bandage / field medical option
4. carry/logistics improvement
5. access/repair tool
6. one specialized combat option
7. first anomaly-related utility near the end of the opening arc

Do not require seven weapon tiers.

## 20. HOME visual progression target

HOME should visibly change across the opening.

### Start

- improvised fire
- scattered supplies
- weak lighting

### Early

- organized storage
- repaired workbench or medical table

### Middle

- generator / better lighting
- NPC work areas
- more deliberate perimeter

### End of opening arc

- recognizable small settlement
- several functional zones
- human signs of routine

The contrast with deteriorating OUTSIDE should be visible in screenshots.

## 21. Co-op opening progression

The first ten hours should support natural role emergence without classes.

Examples:
- one player carries light/tool utility
- one brings stronger combat/medical support
- one scouts while one hauls

Shared expedition goals should not require all players to interact with the same object separately.

Solo completion remains valid.

## 22. Opening boss policy

Do not rush a full boss into Hour 1–3.

A first major encounter should arrive only after the player has learned:

- movement/combat tells
- preparation
- HOME upgrade value
- route/POI logic

A pre-boss event, powerful elite or environmental threat can foreshadow the later boss.

## 23. Mystery policy

Every answer should create a better question.

Bad reveal:

`The Abyss did it.`

Better progression:

- Why are outages centered around certain infrastructure?
- Why does residue appear in sealed locations?
- Why do unrelated survivors report the same sound?
- Why do maps disagree near certain routes?

The mystery should become more specific before it becomes more explained.

## 24. Civilization decline pacing guardrails

### Too fast

- army/government completely gone in opening minutes
- every shop already destroyed
- NPCs immediately know cosmic truth
- monsters everywhere
- constant purple corruption

### Too slow

- many hours where only dialogue says things are worsening
- no POI changes
- no infrastructure consequences
- no gameplay pressure from decline

### Correct pace

Each 1–2 hour band should contain at least one concrete human-world change, while the cosmic explanation remains behind the visible consequences.

## 25. First ten-hour production sequencing

Production should follow dependency, not chronological ambition.

Recommended order:

1. first expedition loop
2. first HOME payoff
3. first capability gate
4. first POI revisit state
5. second distinct POI
6. first NPC service loop
7. first infrastructure world beat
8. third POI / route choice
9. first anomaly mechanic seed
10. end-of-opening transition

This allows the team to prove the campaign grammar before producing the whole campaign.

## 26. Acceptance tests for the opening campaign

A playtester should be able to answer by Hour 10:

- Why is HOME worth returning to?
- Why do I prepare differently for different trips?
- Which places do I remember from earlier states?
- Which survivor do I care about and why?
- What normal system has failed that HOME now replaces?
- What evidence convinced me the crisis is not ordinary?
- What do I want to investigate next?

If these answers are weak, adding more content is not the solution. The existing beats need stronger causality and payoff.

## 27. Non-goals

This blueprint does not require in the first ten hours:

- marriage
- large-scale farming
- dozens of NPCs
- persistent guilds
- multiple huge bosses
- complex skill trees
- full base-defense raids
- complete city simulation
- explicit cosmic lore encyclopedia

## 28. Final opening statement

The first ten hours should feel like watching the edge of normal life recede.

At first the player goes to a convenience store because supplies are short.

Later the same kind of trip becomes dangerous because roads are blocked, power is failing and hostile things occupy familiar places.

Later still the player realizes that the failure is not only social: some places no longer obey the rules they used to.

Through all of this, HOME becomes more organized, more useful and more human.

That contrast is the opening campaign.

# ABYSSAL WAKE — Civilization Decline Narrative Arc V1

Status: Director narrative contract
Scope: Long-form story progression, world-state storytelling, POI/NPC evolution, Awakening pacing
Non-goals: dialogue script library, quest-by-quest implementation, backend persistence design, final boss script

## Core narrative promise

ABYSSAL WAKE does not begin after civilization has already collapsed. The player lives through the collapse.

The emotional trajectory is deliberately asymmetric:

- HOME becomes warmer, safer, more human and more worth protecting.
- OUTSIDE becomes less explainable, less stable and progressively less compatible with ordinary reality.

The game should repeatedly create the feeling: "I remember when this place was normal."

Civilization decline is not background lore. It must change gameplay, routes, resources, services, NPC behavior, audiovisual language and player planning.

## Narrative rule: explanations fail in stages

The story must not announce "Cthulhu" or an equivalent cosmic explanation early.

Early public explanations remain mundane:

- industrial contamination
- animal disease
- infrastructure failure
- extreme weather
- civil unrest
- mass stress
- misinformation
- coastal evacuation

Each explanation must work for a while, then fail under accumulating evidence.

The player should reach the cosmic interpretation after ordinary institutions, experts and personal experience have exhausted simpler explanations.

## Eight-act civilization decline arc

### Act 0 — Ordinary World

Society functions.

- Shops open normally.
- Mobile networks, power, transit and emergency services work.
- NPCs still think in terms of jobs, rent, school, deliveries, traffic and ordinary plans.
- Strange events exist only at the edge of attention.

Early anomalies:

- unusual fog near water
- short radio interference
- missing pets or wildlife migration
- localized power flicker
- isolated sleep disturbances
- contradictory weather reports
- one or two unexplained injuries

Gameplay effect:

The player is not yet a wasteland survivor. Supplies are common, institutions still provide value and danger is localized.

### Act 1 — Disturbance

Normal explanations remain dominant, but uncertainty becomes visible.

World changes:

- police barriers appear around specific sites
- clinics post temporary warnings
- utility crews work longer hours
- some shelves are empty for ordinary supply reasons
- coastal areas close intermittently
- rumors spread faster than official information

NPC behavior:

- some dismiss events
- some become anxious
- some profit from shortages
- professionals still believe systems can recover

Gameplay effect:

The player begins carrying basic supplies because returning home empty-handed becomes inconvenient rather than catastrophic.

### Act 2 — Explanation Failure

Separate incidents begin forming a pattern that official explanations cannot fully contain.

World changes:

- repeated grid instability
- communication outages in the same geographic bands
- hospital overflow
- impossible biological symptoms
- road closures that reopen without explanation
- public notices contradict lived reality

Narrative devices:

- emergency broadcasts remain calm but increasingly vague
- local workers know more than public statements
- records disagree on time, location or casualty counts
- CCTV/radio evidence suggests events happened differently than witnesses remember

Gameplay effect:

Preparation becomes meaningful. The player begins choosing routes, carrying medicine, managing light and planning return windows.

### Act 3 — Local Disorder

Civilian confidence breaks before civilization itself does.

World changes:

- panic buying
- fuel lines
- informal checkpoints
- neighborhood watches
- partial evacuations
- abandoned vehicles
- businesses closing permanently
- public transit becoming unreliable

HOME evolution:

SafeCamp stops being a temporary rest point and begins becoming a real support node.

Useful projects emerge:

- generator
- radio
- medical table
- storage
- cooking station
- workbench
- water handling

Gameplay effect:

Salvage begins replacing ordinary purchasing. Returning home with the right materials starts changing the next expedition.

### Act 4 — Institutional Retreat

The state still exists, but service coverage becomes fragmented.

World changes:

- districts lose routine policing
- emergency response becomes selective
- hospitals triage aggressively
- telecom service becomes intermittent
- power is restored only to priority zones
- official shelters close or relocate

NPC behavior:

People stop asking when life will return to normal and begin asking where they should go next.

Economic transition:

Money still exists, but material reliability becomes more important than nominal price.

High-value categories:

- fuel
- batteries
- antibiotics
- water treatment
- preserved food
- tools
- ammunition
- mechanical parts

Gameplay effect:

The player is now clearly participating in a survival economy.

### Act 5 — Reality Contamination

The crisis can no longer be explained as ordinary collapse.

World changes:

- architecture subtly violates geometry
- reflections show incorrect spaces
- streets reconnect incorrectly
- sound sources do not match distance
- marine growth appears far inland
- weather forms localized repeating patterns
- animals behave with coordinated abnormality
- sleep and waking memory begin crossing

Important restraint:

Do not cover everything in purple tentacles. Familiar reality must remain visible underneath corruption.

The horror is strongest when a normal location is still recognizable.

Gameplay effect:

World knowledge becomes a progression resource. Players need tools, clues and preparation to cross places that are physically present but no longer behave normally.

### Act 6 — Civilizational Fragmentation

Large-scale systems lose practical authority.

The surviving world is now a network of enclaves, camps, isolated professionals, armed groups and abandoned infrastructure.

HOME becomes strategically and emotionally important.

Settlement roles become meaningful:

- mechanic
- medic
- quartermaster
- scavenger
- guide
- cook/fisher
- radio operator

Gameplay effect:

The HOME economy begins replacing lost external services. Players invest in people and infrastructure because civilization no longer provides those functions automatically.

### Act 7 — Revelation / Awakening

The player finally gains enough evidence to understand that the catastrophe is not simply biological, political, extraterrestrial or environmental.

The Abyss is not merely invading the world. The world's rules are being rewritten around its awakening.

Possible revelations should remain partially unknowable:

- previous civilizations encountered the same phenomenon
- certain infrastructure accidentally intersects with older structures
- dreams and physical geography are linked
- the sea/depths are a boundary rather than a location
- the Awakening may be cyclical, induced or accelerated by human activity

Gameplay effect:

Late regions, bosses and anomalies should test both character progression and accumulated world knowledge.

## Awakening stages and gameplay obligations

Each stage must change at least three categories. A stage that only changes art is not a valid stage transition.

Categories:

1. World geometry / routes
2. Enemy ecology / behavior
3. Resource economy
4. NPC state
5. Audio / weather / lighting
6. POI state
7. Available objectives
8. HOME preparation needs

### Stage 0 — Normal

Corruption is local and deniable.

### Stage 1 — Disturbance

Anomalies become repeatable and location-linked.

### Stage 2 — Encroachment

The crisis affects infrastructure, enemy ecology and travel decisions.

### Stage 3 — Revelation

Reality changes are explicit; ordinary explanations fail.

### Stage 4 — Awakening

World events, major bosses and region transformations become possible.

## POI evolution rule

A memorable POI should have a history, not a single static state.

Each important POI should support at least three narrative states over the long game:

- normal / recently abandoned
- survival reuse / disorder
- corrupted / awakened

### Example: MIRE MART

State A — ordinary convenience store

- stocked shelves
- functional refrigerators
- mundane signage
- staff/customer traces

State B — disruption

- shortages
- emergency notices
- damaged glass
- rushed evacuation

State C — survival node / looted site

- improvised barricade
- stripped shelves
- hidden supplies
- signs of conflict

State D — early anomaly

- failing light pattern
- residue
- crawler presence
- impossible refrigeration noise

State E — awakened return

- altered interior logic
- changed loot ecology
- narrative evidence that the player recognizes because they knew the earlier state

The player should feel loss through comparison rather than exposition.

## NPC arc rule

NPCs should begin as ordinary people before becoming "survival roles."

A mechanic should first be a mechanic because that was their job, not because the game needs a crafting vendor.

A medic should first be dealing with ordinary triage before becoming a settlement medical pillar.

Each major NPC arc should include:

- ordinary identity
- first disruption
- personal loss or contradiction
- adaptation
- contribution to HOME
- reaction to undeniable cosmic evidence

Avoid making every NPC an eccentric prophecy-delivering lore character.

## Information hierarchy

The player learns the crisis through four layers:

### Public information

News, emergency alerts, utility notices, public announcements.

### Local information

NPC testimony, neighborhood rumors, worker knowledge, physical evidence.

### Restricted information

Medical logs, infrastructure records, research material, sealed facilities.

### Impossible information

Dreams, repeated symbols, impossible recordings, altered spaces, direct anomaly interaction.

The narrative should move progressively down this hierarchy.

## HOME narrative progression

HOME is the counter-theme to cosmic decline.

As external civilization fails, the player rebuilds small-scale civilization deliberately.

HOME progression should visibly restore functions the outside world is losing:

- light
- food reliability
- medicine
- communication
- repair
- storage
- companionship
- memory
- ritual / routine

This creates the central emotional contrast:

The world is becoming less human while HOME becomes more human because of player action.

## Failure and scarcity rule

Civilization decline should not become constant punishment.

Every new pressure needs:

- readable warning
- preparation option
- partial recovery path
- meaningful player choice

The game should create anxiety through uncertainty and consequence, not random resource deletion.

## Co-op narrative rule

Co-op should change how civilization is rebuilt.

Meaningful shared actions include:

- one player scouts while another carries supplies
- reviving a partner on a failed expedition
- dividing scarce preparation resources
- completing HOME projects together
- witnessing the same POI at different historical states

The story must remain valid solo. Co-op amplifies human connection; it is not required to make the narrative coherent.

## Long-form pacing rule

Do not rush the collapse.

The opening hours should preserve enough normality that later deterioration is measurable.

The player needs repeated exposure to ordinary places before their later transformation becomes emotionally meaningful.

Recommended pacing philosophy:

- early game: curiosity > horror
- early-mid game: uncertainty > danger
- mid game: danger > certainty
- late-mid game: survival + revelation
- late game: awe + dread + irreversible world change

## Anti-patterns

Do not:

- begin with civilization already completely destroyed
- name the cosmic threat immediately
- make every area dark/purple from the start
- use lore dumps instead of changing locations
- transform every NPC into a cultist or prophet
- make every anomaly lethal
- treat Awakening as a simple difficulty slider
- make HOME visually warm but mechanically irrelevant
- use one-time POIs that never evolve

## Vertical-slice narrative target

The first playable slice does not need to implement the full decline arc.

It should prove the pattern in miniature:

1. HOME feels organized and human.
2. The road outside is colder and less controlled.
3. MIRE MART is recognizable as an ordinary modern place.
4. Evidence suggests recent social disruption.
5. Crawler / residue / flicker introduce the first impossible layer.
6. The player returns with useful material or information.
7. HOME gains value from the successful return.
8. The next objective implies that the anomaly is larger than one store.

The slice should end with curiosity and unease, not full cosmic revelation.

## Director acceptance test

A future feature supports this narrative direction only if it strengthens at least one of these:

- remembering what the world used to be
- noticing how it is changing
- preparing for what changed
- bringing value home
- rebuilding a human function
- discovering why ordinary explanations fail

If it does none of these and does not materially improve core combat/co-op usability, it is probably not a priority feature.
